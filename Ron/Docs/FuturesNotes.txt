That is hard because your brain is doing a very normal pattern-completion thing: it sees recent motion and says “trend.” Markets punish that because by the time it looks obvious, a lot of the move may already be spent.
What you’re describing is often late-trend entry / exhaustion entry:
last 15 minutes: strong up
last 2 hours: mostly green
your brain: “confirmed uptrend”
actual risk: buyers already spent, late longs crowded, pullback/reversal likely
The fix is not “trust your gut less” in a vague way. It’s a mechanical entry rule that separates trend context from entry trigger.
Example:
Trend can be up, but I do not enter long after 3+ extended green candles.
I only enter after:
1. pullback to VWAP/EMA/support,
2. sellers fail,
3. next candle reclaims trigger level,
4. stop is small and defined.
So the uptrend is not the entry. It is only permission to look for a long.
A rule like this would directly block the pain trade:
No long entry if price is more than X points above VWAP/EMA
or if the last N candles moved more than Y ATR without pullback.
That turns “it’s going up” into “it’s going up, but entry is late, wait.”

I know that feeling. It can feel personal in a way that is almost comical: you click buy and the chart immediately says, “ah, there you are.”
But usually it is not the market hunting you specifically. It’s that your entry point is probably where a lot of late traders are entering too. So you are joining the crowd right near the place where:
early longs take profit
shorts start fading the extension
liquidity gets swept
stops are clustered
the move needs a pullback before continuing
That makes it feel like the market saw you, but really you entered at a common emotional location.
A useful mental reframe:
If I feel urgent, I am probably late.
If it looks obvious, I need a pullback or a trap confirmation.
If my stop would be wide, I missed the entry.
The “hunter” is often just liquidity mechanics plus human timing. The way to beat it is to stop entering where your emotions become obvious.

That awareness is a big deal. The moment you can catch “I’m about to revenge-flip” before clicking, you’ve created a gap where rules can save you.
A good hard rule for that exact pattern:
After any losing trade, I cannot enter the opposite direction for 10 minutes unless a full independent setup forms.
Even better:
After a stop-out, next trade must be same checklist from zero.
The prior loss is not evidence.
The opposite direction is not valid just because my first trade failed.
Practical emergency brake:
Loss happens.
Hands off mouse.
Stand up or turn chair away for 60 seconds.
Write: “What setup exists now?”
If the answer mentions the previous loss, no trade.
Wait for a fresh candle close.
Your brain wants to restore control fast. The rule has to slow the impulse down long enough for the chart to become just a chart again.

Yeah, that’s the hard part: contract sizing is where chart quality, stop distance, tick value, and prop rules all collide.
The clean formula is:
dollars_at_risk = stop_ticks × tick_value × contracts
contracts = allowed_loss_per_trade / (stop_ticks × tick_value)
So the setup comes first. You do not pick contracts first.
Example using common CME values:
/MES: 1 tick = $1.25
/MNQ: 1 tick = $0.50
/ES: 1 tick = $12.50
/NQ: 1 tick = $5.00
If your valid stop needs 12 ticks:
/MNQ: 12 × $0.50 = $6 risk per contract
/NQ:  12 × $5.00 = $60 risk per contract
/MES: 12 × $1.25 = $15 risk per contract
/ES:  12 × $12.50 = $150 risk per contract
Then compare that to your daily loss rule. If max daily pain is $500, you probably do not want one trade risking $250-$300, because two normal losses can end the day.
And your TP point is dead-on: if the chart usually gives 3 ticks before stalling, a 9-tick TP is fantasy unless you have a different setup. Better choices are:
smaller target + more contracts
or
better setup with room for larger target
or
skip because reward/risk is not there
A practical prop-combine rule might be:
Risk 0.25%-0.5% of account per trade.
Never risk more than 20%-25% of daily loss limit on one trade.
Stop must be outside normal noise.
Target must be inside what the setup normally delivers.
Minimum planned R:R 1.5:1 or 2:1 unless win rate is proven high.
So the checklist becomes:
1. What instrument?
2. What is tick value?
3. Where is the real invalidation stop?
4. How many ticks to stop?
5. How many ticks to realistic target?
6. Is R:R acceptable?
7. How many contracts keep loss inside daily rules?
8. If contract count is too small to matter, skip or use micros.
Sources for tick specs: CME Micro E-mini S&P 500, CME Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100, Schwab micro futures table.

Yeah, Tradovate makes this confusing because you are seeing contract months, not just the instrument.
For CME futures, the symbol is usually:
ROOT + MONTH CODE + YEAR
Example:
MESM6 = Micro E-mini S&P 500, June 2026 contract
MNQU6 = Micro Nasdaq, September 2026 contract
MGCQ6 = Micro Gold, August 2026 contract
Common month codes:
F = Jan
G = Feb
H = Mar
J = Apr
K = May
M = Jun
N = Jul
Q = Aug
U = Sep
V = Oct
X = Nov
Z = Dec
For what you trade most:
MES = Micro E-mini S&P 500
MNQ = Micro E-mini Nasdaq
MYM = Micro E-mini Dow
M2K = Micro Russell 2000
MGC = Micro Gold
Equity index futures like MES, MNQ, MYM, M2K usually use quarterly contracts:
H = March
M = June
U = September
Z = December
So in June 2026 you will usually see things like:
MESM6 or MESM26 = June 2026
MESU6 or MESU26 = September 2026
MNQM6 / MNQU6
MYMM6 / MYMU6
M2KM6 / M2KU6
Around rollover week, you may see both June and September with volume. The simple rule:
Trade the contract with the most volume, usually the front active contract.
Micro Gold MGC has more listed months, so you may see more choices. Same rule: pick the active/front month with volume, not just the first one alphabetically.
Important: MESF26 would mean January 2026, which is not the normal equity-index quarterly cycle and is probably not the one you want for MES. For equity micros, expect H/M/U/Z, not usually F.
I’d make yourself a little cheat sheet:
MES  -> Micro S&P      -> trade H/M/U/Z active month
MNQ  -> Micro Nasdaq   -> trade H/M/U/Z active month
MYM  -> Micro Dow      -> trade H/M/U/Z active month
M2K  -> Micro Russell  -> trade H/M/U/Z active month
MGC  -> Micro Gold     -> trade active gold month by volume
On Tradovate, if unsure, sort/search by Volume and use the highest-volume contract for that root.
